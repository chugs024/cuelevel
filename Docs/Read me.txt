CUELEVEL 1.2
Lossless loudness levelling for event playback

Requires an Apple silicon Mac with macOS 13 or later.

INSTALL
Open the CueLevel DMG and drag CueLevel into Applications. Quit any older version before replacing it.

THE FAST WAY: QUICK LEVEL
1. In the right-hand panel, switch on Quick Level and choose an output folder (for example on your show drive).
2. Drop audio or video files anywhere in the window.
3. Each file is measured, levelled to the target (−18 LUFS by default) and saved into the output folder under its own
   name. No dialogs. Originals are never changed, and existing files are never overwritten ("Cue 12 2.mov").
4. After each batch, "CueLevel Report.csv" and "CueLevel Report.html" in the output folder list every cue: loudness
   before and after, gain applied, and anything that needs attention.

What "lossless" means here
• Video: the picture, subtitles, chapters, metadata and timecode are copied untouched (bit-for-bit).
• Audio: one exact volume change, stored uncompressed (24-bit PCM, or 32-bit float for float sources) at the
  original sample rate. Nothing is compressed, resampled, limited or re-encoded.
• MP4, MOV and M4V videos are saved as MOV, because MP4 cannot carry uncompressed audio. Other video containers stay
  MKV (Resolume may not open MKV, so prefer MOV or MP4 sources). Audio files are saved as WAV.
• Extra audio tracks in a video are copied untouched; the first track is the one levelled.
• If reaching the target would push the loudest peak above the ceiling (−1 dBTP), the cue is kept quieter instead and
  the report says by how much. To reach the target anyway, tick "Allow peak limiting" and choose the most limiting you
  accept (1–6 dB); only those peaks are altered.

WATCH FOLDER
Switch on Watch folder and choose a folder (it must differ from the output folder). Files saved into it afterwards are
added automatically once they have finished copying, and levelled when Quick Level is on. Files already in the folder
when you start watching are left alone.

DETAILED WORK: REVIEW, PROCESSING AND REFERENCES
• Add Files or drop files with Quick Level off to review them one by one. Analyse measures loudness, true peak,
  loudness range and peak-to-average ratio, draws the waveform with short-term loudness over time, and runs
  source checks (clipping, DC offset, out-of-phase stereo, silence at the start or end, surround, extra tracks).
• Choose Audio only, Audio + voice or Voice. Other output types (WAV, FLAC, M4A, video with AAC, Video + WAV copy)
  enable the full processing chain: automatic leveler, peak compression, noise and hum reduction, de-essing,
  harshness control and voice filters. These change the sound by design and are not lossless.
• Original / Processed with Match loudness compares the two at equal loudness.
• Standards menu: Live venue −18, EBU R128 −23, ATSC −24, online video −16, music streaming −14 LUFS.
• Reference library: import your approved references (measurements only). Each analysed cue is compared with the
  references for its content type and given a recommendation — Preserve (gain only) or Gentle correction.
  "Apply each cue's recommendation" does this automatically during batch processing.
• Show Report… saves the CSV and printable HTML report for the current queue at any time.
• Presets, the queue, settings and results are kept between launches in ~/Library/Application Support/CueLevel.
  Removing a cue removes only CueLevel's cached copy.

WHAT CUELEVEL DOES NOT DO
Set the audience volume (that stays at the desk), correct the room, repair existing distortion, separate speech from
music, or remove reverb. Surround sources are downmixed to stereo, which is not lossless.

CLIP GAIN (EVEN OUT VOICES)
Select a cue and click "Clip gain" above the waveform. Drag across the waveform to select a section, then drag its white
line up or down to raise or lower just that section (±24 dB). Drag the orange squares at the top to set the fade-in and
fade-out lengths, and drag the lower part of a section's edge to resize it. To even out speakers: select a stretch that
sounds right and click "Sounds right", then select a quiet or loud stretch and click "Match"; CueLevel measures both
and sets the gain so they are equally loud. Click "Level again" to render. The levelled output includes the edits and
replaces the normal output. Clip gain is volume only (no compression or EQ); the rest of the cue is untouched.

BUILD YOUR REFERENCE CATALOGUE
When a cue sounds right on the PA, select it and choose "Add as an approved reference…" (below the reference checks).
Pick its content type and add a note if you like. CueLevel keeps the cue's measurements (loudness, range, peaks,
peak-to-average ratio, stereo correlation) and a fingerprint of the file, never the recording. Every later cue is
compared with the range of your approved references for its content type. Adding the same file again replaces its entry;
remove entries under References → Catalogue.

UPDATES
CueLevel checks for new versions at launch and twice a day, and offers "Install and reopen" when one is ready. Updates
are installed only if they match the published checksum, are signed by CueLevel's developer and pass macOS Gatekeeper.
Turn automatic checks off in About CueLevel; CueLevel → Check for Updates… checks on demand.

PRIVACY
All processing happens on this Mac. CueLevel never uploads your files and collects no data. When update checks are on,
it reads one small public version file on GitHub; nothing about you or your files is sent.

Third-party components and licences are listed in the Third-party notices folder inside the app
(CueLevel.app/Contents/Resources/Third-party notices).
